Inspiration

        
My friend got me a Kodak Charmera as birthday present (a small keychain camera blind box with a lot of cute filters). I was inspired and decided to make a small camera filter application using the built-in camera on my Mac. I started with simple openCV color conversion functions. As someone deeply interested in film photography, I wanted to go further by recreating the look of classic Kodak film (Vision 3 especially). I turned to professional color science references and gradually expanded my pipeline, refining tone mapping, color grading, and contrast to achieve a more cinematic feel.

Successes and Failures

        
I didn't follow my original pitch of analyzing the computer performance when applying the filter. Eventually, I found filters more interesting to develop. One major failure is when I initially use some parameters to build vision3 the result was very weird, making the center of the frame very reddish. I have to turn to some professional photography website for reference. Meanwhile the implementation is quite math-related and took me a while to fully understand.


        
Final thoughts

        
I learned a lot from the structure of this project. Start from "How do I even open the camera" to actually apply the functions to create different filters. The most interesting part technically is probably that the filters aren't just one-liners; they chain effects (Vision3 does 5 passes) to approximate how real film stock actually responds to light.
